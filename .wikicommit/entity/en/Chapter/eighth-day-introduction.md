The *Introduction* to the Eighth Day is the section that opens the eighth day of the [[Book/decameron]] and precedes the First Tale. It is the briefest of the introductions in the work — a single period — and its function is essentially to mark the resumption of the storytelling after the two-day interruption disposed by [[Person/lauretta]] at the end of the Seventh Day.

## Contents

The rays of the rising light appear "upon the summits of the highest mountains", on Sunday morning, and every shadow has departed, when the queen rises. With her company she first goes some way "over the dewy little grasses"; then, "at the middle of terce", she visits a little church near them and there hears the divine office. Returned home and having eaten "with gladness and with festivity", they sing and dance a while; and whoever wishes, dismissed by the queen, may go to rest.

The sun having passed "the circle of midday", all sit down beside the fair fountain as it pleases the queen, and by her command [[Person/neifile]] begins the storytelling of the day.

## Framing and Significance

Though very brief, the section documents the only explicit religious observance of the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]] outside the deliberations that establish it. Neifile, at the end of the Second Day, and Lauretta, at the end of the Seventh, had both justified the suspension of the storytelling by the reverence due to Friday and Saturday; here the text shows the company which, its own order resumed, devotes Sunday morning to the divine office before returning to the telling.

The theme that governs the day had been fixed by Lauretta two days earlier: the tricks ([[DefinedTerm/beffa]]) that day by day woman plays on man, man on woman, or the one man on the other.
