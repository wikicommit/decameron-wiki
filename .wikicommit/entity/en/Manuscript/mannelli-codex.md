The *Mannelli Codex* is the apograph of the [[Book/decameron]] transcribed by Francesco Mannelli, identified by the shelfmark Codice Laurenziano Pluteo XLII 1 and dated 13 August 1384. It is one of the ancient witnesses of the work, alongside the Hamilton autograph ([[Manuscript/hamilton-90]]), which the encyclopaedic entry cites only through the bibliography and the critical editions founded upon it.

Its principal interest for the history of the text lies in the title that is read there, reported in full: "Comincia il libro chiamato Decameron, cognominato prencipe Galeotto. Nel quale si contengono cento novelle in diece dì decte da septe donne & da tre giovani huomini." It is from this formula that the attestation of the subtitle *Prencipe Galeotto* is drawn, alluding to the character of the Breton cycle who acted as intermediary in love between Lancelot and Guinevere and to the Dantean verse *Galeotto fu 'l libro e chi lo scrisse*.

## Description and history

The encyclopaedic source does not indicate the place of copying, nor does it describe the material and the layout of the codex, and the place of production therefore remains unrecorded. Of the copyist, Francesco Mannelli, the entry says nothing but his name, referring to the entry dedicated to him by Arianna Terzi in the *Dizionario biografico degli italiani* (vol. 69, Rome, Istituto dell'Enciclopedia Italiana, 2007).

The text of the codex was later made the foundation of an eighteenth-century edition, printed at Lucca in 1761 with the title *Il Decameron, tratto dall'ottimo testo scritto da Fran.co d'Amaretto Mannelli sull'originale dell'autore*.

## Textual relevance

The *Decameron* possesses a manuscript tradition long studied by twentieth-century philology: besides the Hamilton autograph, placed by [[Person/vittore-branca]] at the foundation of the critical edition published by the Accademia della Crusca in 1976, the entry signals the existence of studies dedicated to the relation between the Berlin manuscript and the Mannelli codex, among them the work of Oscar Hecker on the kinship between the two witnesses.
