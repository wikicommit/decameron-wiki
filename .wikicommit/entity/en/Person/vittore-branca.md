Vittore Branca was a philologist and one of the greatest scholars of [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]] of the later twentieth century, together with Giuseppe Billanovich. In the course of the twentieth century his critical-philological studies, together with those of Carlo Dionisotti and of Billanovich himself, brought to a culmination a flourishing season begun by the favourable judgements of Ugo Foscolo, Francesco de Sanctis and Vincenzo Crescini, directed at giving a more real image to that "boccaccesque" one fastened upon Boccaccio in the last centuries.

## Activity and contributions

Branca contributed, in collaboration with Pier Giorgio Ricci, to certifying the autograph character of the codex [[Manuscript/hamilton-90]], bearing the [[Book/decameron]], which he himself had discovered.

In his book *Boccaccio medievale* he tended to emphasise the medieval mentality of the man of Certaldo, upon which are based the values, the images and the scenes of the tales — a position opposed to that of Francesco de Sanctis, who had gone so far as to define Boccaccio the first writer detached from the medieval mentality. Gianfranco Contini expressed a judgement analogous to Branca's, glossing that "today Boccaccio appears on the one hand of medieval and retrospective culture, on the other a good Italian deuteragonist of that aristocratic movement which was Humanism".

Branca is among the scholars who define Boccaccio the greatest European prose writer of his time, a versatile writer under the sign of [[DefinedTerm/boccaccian-experimentalism]]. He maintained besides that the signature "Johannes de Certaldo" used by Boccaccio would indicate the homeland of the family rather than the physical place of birth, and observed that the propagation of the *Decameron* "is more European than Italian". On the relationship between Boccaccio and [[Person/petrarch]], Branca invited scholars to go beyond the simple pairing *praeceptor*-*discipulus* and to observe instead the "convergence in problems, in interests, in analogous solutions, stylistic ones too: intertextuality, as it has since been called".

In 1963 he founded the journal "Studi sul Boccaccio", today promoted by the [[Organization/ente-nazionale-giovanni-boccaccio]].
