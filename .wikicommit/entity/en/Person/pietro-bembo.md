Pietro Bembo, Italian poet and future cardinal, is the author of the sixteenth-century consecration of [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]] as a linguistic model: in the sixteenth century he raised the Boccaccian style to a model of Italian prose, with determining consequences for the subsequent literary tradition.

## The Prose della volgar lingua

The consecration came in 1525, when Bembo, with his *Prose della volgar lingua*, delineated the [[Book/decameron]] as the model of prose. In the work he argues that the language of past seasons may be better than that of the present:

> "But how many times does it happen that the manner of the language of past seasons is better than that of the present is [...] and we shall likewise do much better if we discourse in our pages with the style of Boccaccio and of Petrarch than we shall do in discoursing with our own, for without any doubt they discoursed much better than we discourse."

Bembo's judgement thus places Boccaccio and [[Person/petrarch]] as the two reference models of the Italian literary language. The fortune of the two proposals was, however, different: while that of the Petrarchan lyric would last until the nineteenth century, giving rise to the phenomenon of Petrarchism, Boccaccio suffered a sharp condemnation on the part of the Council of Trent on account of the "immoral" contents present in many tales.
