Dante Alighieri is the author of the *Divina Commedia* and the model that [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]] venerated all his life. With [[Person/petrarch]] and Boccaccio he is one of the [[DefinedTerm/three-crowns]] of Italian literature.

## Boccaccio's Dantean cult

Boccaccio had occasion to know Dante and his work from his earliest youth, thanks to his contact with Margherita and Filippa de' Mardoli: his father had in fact married, shortly before 1320, the noblewoman Margherita de' Mardoli, related to the Portinari family. Having then perfected himself at the school of Cino da Pistoia, who had maintained friendly relations with Alighieri, Boccaccio showed a precocious approach to the poetics of the venerated model: already in the *Caccia di Diana* the presence of Dantean tercets attests to it, and the echo will be prolonged as far as the aged *Corbaccio*, where the presence of the wood and of the vision refer back to the infernal setting of the poem.

Boccaccio gave rise to Dantean criticism and philology. He copied with his own hand three codices of the *Commedia*, preparing a manuscript edition of it whose text he critically corrected, and wrote the *Trattatello in laude di Dante Alighieri*, fundamental for Dante's biography. He also held *lectiones magistrales* upon the cantos of the *Inferno* in the Badia Fiorentina, which stopped at the exegesis of the seventeenth canto on account of his abrupt physical decline and flowed into the *Esposizioni sopra la Commedia*. In his Dantean commentary Boccaccio often intervenes upon Dante's text, deliberately altering it or interpreting it according to the poetic-narrative context of the passage analysed.

The approach to the humanistic mentality and the cult of Petrarch did not divert Boccaccio from the will to spread the cult of Dante at [[Place/florence]], even if the more than optimistic judgement cooled during the humanist phase, after he had observed Petrarch's superiority in the Latin tongue. In August-September of 1350 Boccaccio was sent to Ravenna to carry to Sister Beatrice, Dante's daughter, ten gold florins in the name of the captains of the company of Orsanmichele; during that embassy he will probably have gathered information concerning the beloved poet. Already earlier, during the Ravenna sojourn of 1345-46, according to what Marco Santagata recalls, he had known some old friends of Dante from the exile, such as Dino Perini and Pietro Giardini.

## Presence in the Decameron

The subtitle of the [[Book/decameron]], *Il principe Galeotto*, alludes to the celebrated line "Galeotto fu 'l libro e chi lo scrisse", referred by Dante to the book read by Paolo and Francesca (*Inf.* V 137). On the conceptual plane, Francesco de Sanctis defined the *Decameron* "the earthly Commedia": in Boccaccio Fortune is by now completely unbound from supernatural forces, unlike what happens in Dante, who reflects upon it in the seventh canto of the *Inferno*, and is left to be managed and confronted by the protagonist.
