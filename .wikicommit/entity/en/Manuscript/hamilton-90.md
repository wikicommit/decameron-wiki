The *Hamilton 90 Manuscript* is the codex of the [[Book/decameron]] which [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]] transcribed in 1370, in the last years of his life. It is one of the autograph codices of the work and is preserved at the Staatsbibliothek of Berlin.

## Description and history

The codex was discovered by [[Person/vittore-branca]]. The philologist then contributed, in collaboration with Pier Giorgio Ricci, to certifying its autograph character.

The transcription belongs to a phase in which, alongside his humanistic production in Latin, Boccaccio continued to cultivate his love for vernacular poetry: in those same years he prepared a manuscript edition of the *Divina Commedia*, critically correcting its text, and wrote the *Trattatello in laude di Dante*.

## Textual significance

The autograph character of the codex makes it a witness of the first importance for the textual tradition of the *Decameron*: it is a witness prepared by the author himself more than twenty years after the elaboration of the work, begun during the [[Event/black-death-1348]] and completed in 1351 — or, according to more modern studies, in 1355. Its identification by Branca and Ricci belongs to that season of twentieth-century philological studies which redefined the critical image of the man of Certaldo.
