The *Introduction* to the Second Day is the section that opens the second of the ten days of the [[Book/decameron]] and precedes the First Tale. It is among the briefest in the work: it consists of a single connecting period, with no narrative matter of its own beyond the description of the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]]'s day and the command that sets the storytelling going.

## Contents

The sun has already carried the light of the new day everywhere and the birds are singing "upon the green boughs" when the women and the three young men rise and enter the gardens, treading with slow step upon the dewy grass and going about to divert themselves for a long space, making themselves "fair garlands". The day retraces the previous one: having eaten in the cool and danced a little, they go to rest; risen after nones, as it pleases the queen they come into the "cool little meadow" and dispose themselves to sit about her.

The queen [[Person/filomena]] — "shapely and of a very pleasing aspect", crowned with her laurel garland — remains a while in silence and looks into the face of the whole company, then commands [[Person/neifile]] to make a beginning of the tales; and she, "without making any excuse", begins gladly to speak.

## Framing and Significance

The section shows in action, for the first time since its institution, the settled working of the [[DefinedTerm/frame-narrative]]: the sovereignty has passed from hand to hand at the end of the previous day, the laurel garland appears as the visible sign of the lordship, and the storyteller who opens the day is designated by the sovereign rather than offering themselves. The very brevity of the passage is significant: the order having been established in the Conclusion of the First Day, the frame no longer needs to be explained and is reduced to the scansion of the hours — walk, meal, rest, gathering about the queen — that will repeat itself, with variations, on all the days that follow.
