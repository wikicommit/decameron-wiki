The *Filostrato* is a short poem in octaves by [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]], dated to 1335 and composed in the Neapolitan period. The title, literally, should mean in Boccaccio's approximate Greek "vanquished by love".

## Plot

The work narrates the tragic story of Troilus, son of the Trojan king Priam, who had fallen in love with the Greek princess Criseida. Following an exchange of prisoners, the woman returns to the Greek camp and forgets Troilus. When Criseida subsequently falls in love with Diomedes, Troilus despairs and goes to meet his death at the hands of Achilles.

## Composition and Significance

In this work the author engages directly with the preceding tradition of the *cantari*, fixing the parameters for a new type of octave that would be essential to the whole of Italian literature down to the seventeenth century. The language adopted is difficult and lofty, unlike that found in the [[Book/filocolo]], which is instead highly superabundant. Together with the [[Book/teseida]], the *Caccia di Diana* and the *Filocolo*, the *Filostrato* belongs to the output that Boccaccio produced in [[Place/naples]] while frequenting the Angevin court and occupying himself with literature.
