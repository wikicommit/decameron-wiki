Geoffrey Chaucer, author of *The Canterbury Tales*, is indicated as the first great English man of letters and poet and as a key figure of English literature. He is the principal witness to the European fortune of [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]] in the Anglo-Saxon world.

## The relationship with the Decameron

According to what [[Person/vittore-branca]] had occasion to say, the propagation of the [[Book/decameron]] "is more European than Italian": the circulation of the work in France, Spain, Germany and above all in England was without precedent. In the Anglo-Saxon world Boccaccio was more than a simple model: he was the inspirer of the milestone that inspired Chaucer.

*The Canterbury Tales* are structured in the same way as the *Decameron*, both from the point of view of the literary genre and from that of the contents, even if Chaucer collected no more than 24 tales, instead of the 100 hoped for. Boccaccio's success in Europe was not bound, however, only to the *Decameron*, but also to works considered "minor", such as the *De casibus virorum illustrium* and the [[Book/filocolo]], and, among the erudite, to the Latin encyclopaedias.
