The *Introduction* to the Fifth Day is the short section that opens the fifth day of the [[Book/decameron]] and precedes the First Tale. Like the other introductions of the central part of the work, it has no narrative matter of its own: it records the scansion of the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]]'s hours and the passage to the storytelling.

## Contents

The east is already all white and the rising rays have made it bright "throughout our hemisphere" when [[Person/fiammetta]], stirred by the sweet songs of the birds that sang gladly upon the little trees in the first hour of the day, rises and has all the others and the three young men called. Having gone down with gentle step to the fields, she goes about diverting herself with the company through the wide plain upon the dewy grass, discoursing "of one thing and another", until the sun has risen somewhat.

Feeling that the sun's rays are already growing warm, the queen turns her steps back towards their lodging, where she has the light weariness restored "with excellent wines and with comfits"; then the company diverts itself through the delightful garden until the hour of eating. This being come, and everything being made ready by the "most discreet steward", they first sing "some stampita and a little ballata or two", then they eat; and this being done in due order and with gladness, without forgetting the order taken as to dancing, they dance some little dances with instruments and with songs.

## Framing and Significance

The queen then dismisses everyone until the hour of sleep is past: some go to sleep, others remain at their diversion through the fair garden. But all, a little after nones, gather as it pleases the queen near the fountain "after the accustomed manner". Fiammetta seats herself *pro tribunali* and, looking towards [[Person/panfilo]], smiling she lays it upon him to make a beginning "of the happy tales"; and he willingly disposes himself to it.

The expression with which the text describes the queen's posture — seated as in a tribunal — gives the measure of how far the sovereignty of the [[DefinedTerm/frame-narrative]] has by now settled into recognisable forms; and the adjective with which the tales are announced recalls the theme fixed at the end of the previous day, that of lovers who after harsh mishaps came to a happy outcome.
