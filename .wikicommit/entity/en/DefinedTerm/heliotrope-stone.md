The **heliotrope** is, in the definition given by the false lapidary of [[ShortStory/calandrino-and-the-heliotrope]], "a stone […] of exceedingly great virtue, for whoever carries it upon himself, as long as he holds it, is seen by no other person where he is not". The name is presented in the text as a term from the jargon of lapidaries — "which we lapidaries call heliotrope" — that is, as a technical word belonging to a specialised body of knowledge about the virtues of stones.

## Usage

In the tale the heliotrope functions as the bait of the [[DefinedTerm/beffa]]: the credibility of the power attributed to it rests not on a demonstration but on the simulated authority of the one who names it, who speaks of it "so convincingly […] as though he had been a solemn and great lapidary" and with "a steady face and without laughing".

When asked for practical directions, the trickster adds only those details necessary for the search to begin and at the same time to be incapable of failing in any recognisable way: the stone is said to be found in the Mugnone, to be of variable size — "some are larger, some smaller" — and of a colour "almost like black". These are traits generic enough that any dark stone on the riverbed matches the description, and it is on this indeterminacy that the misunderstanding rests: the target indiscriminately gathers every black stone he sees, "until we chance upon it", and then derives the conviction of having found it not from an examination of the stone but from the feigned behaviour of his companions.
