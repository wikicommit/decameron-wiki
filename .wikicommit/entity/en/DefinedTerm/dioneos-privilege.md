*Dioneo's privilege* is the dispensation from the rule of the obligatory theme that [[Person/dioneo]] requests and obtains in the Conclusion of the First Day of the [[Book/decameron]], at the very moment when that rule is established: he asks "as a special grace" for a gift, to be confirmed "for as long as our company shall last", namely not to be constrained "by this law" of having to tell a tale according to the given proposal, if he does not wish to, but rather whichever one he most pleases to tell.

## Usage

The request is formulated immediately after [[Person/filomena]], elected queen of the Second Day, has for the first time restricted the subject matter of the storytelling to a theme announced in advance. Dioneo waits until all the others have fallen silent and prefaces his request by saying that he does not want it believed that he asks the grace "as a man who has no tales at hand": for this reason he declares himself "content always to be the last to speak". The privilege therefore has a price, and is at once an exemption and a fixed position in the order of the storytellers.

The queen, who knows Dioneo as a "merry and jesting man", perceives "perfectly well" that he asks for the gift only in order to be able to cheer the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]] with a laughable tale, should it grow weary of discoursing; and with the consent of the others she gladly grants it to him. The concession is thus not a mere indulgence toward an undisciplined member, but an institution of the [[DefinedTerm/frame-narrative]] deliberately adopted for its usefulness to the company.

## Related Terms

The privilege can be understood only in relation to the rule from which it departs, the theme set by the king or queen of each day, and it stands alongside the other institutions established by the brigata in the same section: the rotating succession of the laurel garland and the fixed hour at which each day closes. See also [[DefinedTerm/ballata]].
