The *Introduction* to the Sixth Day is the section that opens the sixth day of the [[Book/decameron]] and precedes the First Tale. It is the only introduction in the central part of the work to contain an autonomous narrative episode: the altercation between two servants of the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]], which the text itself signals as an unprecedented event — "there befell a thing that had not yet befallen there".

## Contents

The moon, being in the middle of the sky, has lost her rays when the queen [[Person/elissa]] rises and has the company called. Ranging over the dew, they go some way from the fair palace holding "various discourses", disputing "of the greater and the lesser beauty of the tales recounted" and renewing their laughter at the cases recited in them, until the sun begins to grow warm and all turn back towards the house. The tables being set and everything strewn "with fragrant little herbs and with fair flowers", they eat before the heat rises further; then, some little songs having been sung, some go to sleep, some to play at chess and at tables, while [[Person/dioneo]] together with [[Person/lauretta]] sings "of Troilus and of Cressida".

The hour being come to return "to consistory", all dispose themselves to sit about the fountain; but just as the queen is about to command the first tale, "a great noise" is heard, made in the kitchen by the maidservants and the household. The steward being called and asked who was shouting, he answers that the noise is between [[Person/licisca]] and [[Person/tindaro]], but that he does not know the cause, having just then arrived to quiet them.

The queen has them both brought and asks the cause of the quarrel. Tindaro wishing to answer, Licisca interrupts him — "Look at this beast of a man, who dares, where I am, to speak before me! Let me speak" — and sets out her own version: Tindaro claims to instruct her about Sicofante's wife, maintaining with an obscene metaphor that she was a virgin at her wedding, whereas she affirms the contrary; and more generally he believes "that young women are so foolish" as to waste their time waiting for father and brothers, who six times out of seven delay marrying them off by three or four years. She concludes by affirming that she has "no neighbour who went to her husband a maid", and that she knows well "how many and what tricks" married women play on their husbands.

While she speaks, the women laugh so much "that all their teeth might have been drawn out", and the queen imposes silence upon her in vain "a good six times". The speech being ended, the queen laughingly entrusts the question to Dioneo that he may give "final sentence" upon it at the end of the tales; but he pronounces sentence on the spot, without hearing anything further, that Licisca is right and that Tindaro "is a beast". Licisca laughs at this and mocks her adversary again, until the queen, "with an ill countenance", imposes silence upon her, threatening to have her "whipped", and sends them both away. The text observes that, without that intervention, the company would have had "nothing else to do" all day "than attend to her". The two having gone, the queen lays it upon [[Person/filomena]] to make a beginning of the tales.

## Framing and Significance

The episode has a structural function that exceeds its comic tone. It introduces into the closed and regulated world of the [[DefinedTerm/frame-narrative]] a voice foreign to the company of the ten, and moreover a voice that the queen's sovereignty cannot silence: the authority that governs the storytelling proves effective upon the storytellers and not upon the servants. The very content of the quarrel then anticipates the matter of the Seventh Day, which at the end of this day Dioneo will avowedly draw from Licisca's words.

The placing is furthermore significant with respect to the day's theme, fixed by Elissa at the end of the Fifth Day: the day of witticisms and ready retorts ([[DefinedTerm/ready-retort]]) opens with a display of ready speech conducted by someone who takes no part in the brigata's literary game.
