Leonardo Salviati — whose name is attested also in the form *Lionardo* — edited in 1582 a second censored edition of the [[Book/decameron]], subsequent to that of the Deputies of 1573. It seems that it was Salviati himself who pressed the Roman curia, by means of his protector Jacopo Buoncompagni, to ask for a new censorship of the work.

## Role in the emendation of 1582

The source rejects as unfounded the hypothesis according to which the new emendation would have been rendered necessary because the Deputies had revealed a certain negligence on the ground of morality, above all sexual, letting the lasciviousness of the text pass too freely. In reality Salviati's *Decameron* appears, rather than a true edition founded upon the results of original research, a correction of the preceding edition.

From this derives the substantial difference with respect to [[Person/vincenzo-borghini]]'s intervention: while the Deputies limited themselves to cutting, Salviati modified, censoring not only the text but also its reading. He had recourse indeed to marginal glosses in order openly to carry out a function of mediation between the text and the reader and to impose a univocal interpretation. His operation spared 48 tales and modified 52. On the affair as a whole see [[DefinedTerm/decameron-censorship]].
