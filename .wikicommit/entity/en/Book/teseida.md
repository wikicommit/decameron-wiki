The *Teseida delle nozze d'Emilia* is an epic poem in octaves by [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]], composed in the Neapolitan period and dated to 1339-41, indicated elsewhere in the source as 1339-1340. It recalls the deeds of Theseus, who fights against Thebes and the Amazons, thus drawing on the Theban cycle.

## Plot

The work constitutes the very first instance, in the literary history of the Italian language, of an epic poem in the vernacular, and Boccaccio's tendency to isolate sentimental narrative nuclei is already manifest in it: the true centre of the narrative in fact turns out to be the love of the Theban prisoners Arcita and Palemone, close friends with one another, for Emilia, queen of the Amazons and sister-in-law of Theseus. The duel between the two lovers ends with the death of Arcita and the marriage of Palemone and Emilia.

## Composition and Significance

The *Teseida* belongs, together with the [[Book/filocolo]], the [[Book/filostrato]] and the *Caccia di Diana*, to the works that Boccaccio composed in [[Place/naples]] while he frequented the Angevin court and occupied himself with literature, writing both in Latin and in the vernacular. The tendency to isolate sentimental narrative nuclei observable in it may be traced back to [[DefinedTerm/boccaccian-experimentalism]], the versatility with which the author reworks heterogeneous literary materials to produce original works.
