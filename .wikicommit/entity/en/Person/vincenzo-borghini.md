Vincenzo Borghini is the figure who emerges among the *Deputies*, the Florentine experts to whom the Inquisition committed in 1573 the task of "setting in order" the text of the [[Book/decameron]]. The source recognises him as the true promoter of the [[DefinedTerm/decameron-censorship]].

## Role in the emendation of 1573

His name recurs in both the reconstructions proposed for the composition of the Deputies, upon whose identity there exists no agreement: the first hypothesis counts three of them — Borghini, Pierfrancesco Cambi and Sebastiano Antinori; the second four — Borghini, Sebastiano Antinori, Agnolo Guicciardini and Antonio Benivieni.

Having received from the Church of Rome the *Decameron* marked in the passages to be modified, the Deputies proceeded to the defence of the text with cultural, traditional, philological and rhetorical arguments, attempting to save what could be saved: to them fell the linguistic and philological specialisation, while the censorship proper fell directly to the Church of Rome. The distinguishing trait of their intervention, with respect to the subsequent one of [[Person/leonardo-salviati]], is that Borghini's Deputies limited themselves to cutting, intervening upon the text and not upon its reading. The Deputies' *Decameron* was shortly afterwards prohibited by the Inquisition itself and therefore knew but a single edition.
