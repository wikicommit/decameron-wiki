The *Elegia di Madonna Fiammetta* is a prose romance by [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]] divided into nine chapters, composed between 1343 and 1344, during his second Florentine period. It tells of a Neapolitan lady abandoned and forgotten by the young Florentine Panfilo.

## Plot

Panfilo's absence causes the protagonist great torment, heightened by the fact that [[Person/fiammetta]] is married and must conceal from her husband the cause of her unhappiness. The work takes the form of a long letter addressed to women in love, and the protagonist's lengthy confession allows for a minute psychological introspection.

## Composition and Significance

The story is narrated from the woman's point of view, a decidedly innovative element with respect to a literary tradition in which woman had been the object and not the subject of love: she is no longer the shadow and projection of man's passion, but an agent in the love story. The [[Book/decameron]] too would be explicitly dedicated by Boccaccio to women.

The work belongs to the group of texts written during his stay in [[Place/florence]], together with the *Comedia delle ninfe fiorentine* (1341-1342), the *Amorosa visione* (1342-1343) and the *Ninfale fiesolano* (1344-1346).
