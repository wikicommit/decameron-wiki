The *Introduction* to the Ninth Day is the section that opens the ninth day of the [[Book/decameron]] and precedes the First Tale. It is the day without a theme, the one [[Person/emilia]] had left free at the end of the Eighth Day; and the section is notable for a detail that breaks, alone among the central introductions, the separation between the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]]'s retreat and the plague-stricken city from which it fled.

## Contents

The light has already changed the eighth heaven "from azure to sky-blue" and the little flowers are beginning to rise up through the meadows when Emilia rises and has her companions and the young men called. Having set out behind the queen's slow steps, they go as far as a little wood not far from the palace and enter it.

Here they see the animals — "such as roe deer, stags and others" — awaiting them "no otherwise [...] than if they had become fearless or tame", and the text gives the reason for it: they are "almost secure from the hunters because of the overhanging pestilence". Approaching now one and now another as though they were about to catch them, and making them run and leap, the company takes its diversion for some space; but the sun already rising high, it seems to all time to return.

They come back garlanded with oak leaves, with their hands full of fragrant herbs or of flowers; and the text comments with a phrase that joins their gladness to the death that surrounds them: whoever had met them "could have said nothing else save: — Either these will not be vanquished by death or it will slay them glad".

## Framing and Significance

Come to the palace "singing and chattering and jesting", they find everything set out in due order and their household glad and festive. Having rested a while, they do not go to table before six little songs have been sung, "one gladder than the other"; then, water having been given for their hands, the steward sets them at table according to the queen's pleasure, and all eat merrily. Risen, they give themselves for some space to carolling and to playing, and then, at the queen's command, whoever wishes goes to rest. The accustomed hour being come, each gathers in the usual place to discourse, and the queen, looking at [[Person/filomena]], tells her to make a beginning of the tales of the present day.

The detail of the animals devoid of fear has an importance that exceeds the note of landscape: it is the sign, within the locus amoenus of the countryside, of the effects of the [[Event/black-death-1348]] upon ordinary life: the hunting has ceased because the hunters have ceased. The frame, which since the First Day had excluded from the retreat every piece of news that was not glad, here lets the very cause of the retreat surface in the indirect form of a wood too quiet.
