Dioneo is one of the three young men of the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]] of the [[Book/decameron]], together with Filostrato and Panfilo. His name has been interpreted as "the lustful one", from Dione, mother of Venus; [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]] called himself *spurcissimus dyoneus* in an early letter.

## Dioneo's privilege

To Dioneo alone, on account of his young age, it is granted not to observe the theme established by the king or queen of the day; in exchange he must always tell his tale last. This exemption, known as "Dioneo's privilege", is one of the exceptions Boccaccio inserts into his otherwise precise and orderly design, and concerns the regulation that the brigata gives itself in the [[DefinedTerm/frame-narrative]] of the work.

His inclination towards licentious themes finds a counterweight within the group: at the end of the Fifth Day [[Person/elissa]] firmly rebukes him to prevent him from introducing obscene themes into the ballata. It is moreover Dioneo who, as king of the Seventh Day, fixes the theme that [[Person/lauretta]] will take up and modify on the following day.

## His tales

The privilege is verified in the text of the philological edition at the very close of the work: the king's long tale being ended, Dioneo knows that to him alone the speaking remains, and he narrates [[ShortStory/griselda]], the tenth tale of the Tenth Day. Here too is measured the thematic freedom the privilege entails, for he opens by dissenting from the day he is closing: after a day devoted — he observes — to kings, sultans and suchlike folk, he announces that he wishes to discourse of a marquis, "not a magnificent thing but a mad brutishness", and adds that he counsels no one to follow it, "for it was a great pity that good came of it to that man".

## The privilege in the text of the philological edition

The granting of the privilege is read in the Conclusion of the First Day, immediately after [[Person/filomena]], elected queen, has instituted the obligatory theme. Dioneo waits until all the others have fallen silent and asks "as a special grace" a gift to hold good "for as long as our company shall last": not to be constrained to tell a tale according to the proposal given, if he does not wish, but the one that shall best please him. And so that it may not be believed that he asks it "as a man who has no tales to hand", he declares himself "content always to be the last who discourses". The motivation the text attributes to the granting is its usefulness to the company: the queen, knowing him for "a merry and festive man", perceives "excellently well" that he asks it only in order to be able to gladden the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]] with a laughable tale should it be weary of discoursing (see [[DefinedTerm/dioneos-privilege]]).

## His reign

Dioneo governs the Seventh Day. At the end of the Sixth [[Person/elissa]] sets the crown upon his head, saying: "It is time, Dioneo, that you prove somewhat what a charge it is to have ladies to govern and to guide". He answers laughing that kings far dearer than he have already been seen — "I speak of the kings at chess" — and that he will reign as he knows how.

The theme he fixes derives avowedly from the altercation heard that morning: had "mistress [[Person/licisca]]" not come there a little before, he says, he doubts he would have laboured "a good while" to find matter. Leaving aside the first part of what she affirmed, "which is childish stuff", he establishes that they discourse "of the tricks which, either for love or for their own salvation, women have already played upon their husbands" (see [[DefinedTerm/beffa]]). To some of the ladies the matter seems unseemly and they beg him to change it, but Dioneo defends it: the time is such that, so long as men and women guard themselves from acting dishonestly, "every discourse is permitted"; "the judges have left the tribunals", divine and human laws are silent, and "ample licence to preserve life is granted to everyone". He adds that the brigata has been from the very first day "most seemly", and that indeed whoever knew them to be reluctant to discourse of such things "would perhaps suspect that you were guilty in this regard".
