Pampinea is one of the seven young women of the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]] of the [[Book/decameron]]. Her name means "the flourishing one", and she is a character already present in the *Commedia delle ninfe fiorentine*, an early work of [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]].

In the *Decameron* Pampinea represents the image of a new and independent woman. It is she who proposes to her friends that they flee the city, and she again who suggests to the three young men that they act as escorts to the company.

## Role in the brigata

Once arrived on the hills of [[Place/fiesole]], Pampinea is named queen by the brigata and assigns duties to each of them, so as to maintain order and balance within the group. It is she again who proposes that tales be told each day, naming a king or a queen who must decide the theme of the day: from this proposal descends the framework of the [[DefinedTerm/frame-narrative]] upon which the work is built.

## Her tales

The tales told by Pampinea take up themes dear to Boccaccio, such as that of fortune ([[DefinedTerm/fortune-and-nature]]), of love and of cunning.

Considerable is the preamble to the tenth tale of the First Day, in which Boccaccio, through the mouth of Pampinea, maintains that the witticism ([[DefinedTerm/ready-retort]]), being brief, is better suited to women, even though only a few are able to understand it: most of them, indeed, spend part of their time upon the care of the body and speak little, justifying their silence by purity of soul.

## The end of her reign

The text of the philological edition shows the term of her lordship. The sun already bowed towards vespers and the tales finished, Pampinea says pleasantly to her companions that nothing further remains for her governance to do "save to give you a new queen". In doing so she also establishes the hour at which the following days are to begin: since whoever does not take "somewhat of time beforehand" does not seem able to provide well for what is to come, and so that whatever the new queen shall determine may be made ready, she judges that the following days ought to begin at that hour. She therefore designates [[Person/filomena]], "a most discreet young woman", and rising to her feet draws off the laurel garland and reverently places it upon her head.
