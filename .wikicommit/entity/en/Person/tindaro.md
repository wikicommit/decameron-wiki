Tindaro is one of the manservants in the service of the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]] in the [[Book/decameron]]. He appears at the opening of the Sixth Day as the counterpart of [[Person/licisca]] in the "great uproar" that arises from the kitchen and that interrupts, in the only such case in the work, the beginning of the telling of tales.

## Background

Both being led before queen [[Person/elissa]], who asks what is the cause of their noise, Tindaro would answer first, but Licisca prevents him and takes the word in his place. His reasons, consequently, reach the reader only through the account his adversary gives of them: according to Licisca he presumes to instruct her about Sicofante's wife, maintaining that she was a virgin on her wedding night, and more generally "wants to teach me about women, as though I were born yesterday".

He is not permitted to reply. Called upon by the queen, [[Person/dioneo]] gives sentence without hearing further, declaring that Licisca is in the right and that Tindaro "is a beast"; and when Licisca draws from this an occasion to mock him again, the queen imposes silence upon her and sends them both away.

## Role in the frame

Outside the altercation, Tindaro appears in the company as a musician. At the end of the Sixth Day, king Dioneo — "who was in good humour" — has him called and commands him to bring out his bagpipe, to the sound of which he has many dances performed, until, a great part of the night having passed, he bids everyone go to sleep.
