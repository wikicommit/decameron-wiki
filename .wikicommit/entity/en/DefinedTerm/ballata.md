The *ballata* — called in the text also "ballatetta" or "canzone" — is the lyric composition that, sung and danced at once, closes the day of the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]] in the [[Book/decameron]]. The manner of performance is collective and articulated: the person who rules the day commands that a dance be taken up and that someone sing; one companion leads the dance, another intones the text, and the company answers.

## Usage

In the Conclusion of the First Day the performance is described in its particulars: having had the instruments brought after supper, the queen [[Person/filomena]] commands "that a dance be taken up", and while [[Person/lauretta]] leads it, [[Person/emilia]] sings the ballata "amorously", "assisted by [[Person/dioneo]]'s lute". At the end the text records everyone's participation: "This little ballata being ended, to which all had gladly answered". The ballata is therefore sung by a single voice but with a choral answer, and it is accompanied by an instrument and by a dance distinct from the singing. The pattern repeats at the close of all ten days, with the reigning sovereign designating each time who is to sing and who is to lead the dance.

The text also notes that the content of the ballata is not a matter of indifference to those who hear it: of the first it is said that, although gladly received by all, it "made some ponder much upon its words". After the ballata some further "little carols" are performed before the day is declared at an end.

## The Ballate of the Days

- **First Day** — "Io son sí vaga della mia bellezza", sung by [[Person/emilia]] while [[Person/lauretta]] leads the dance: the female voice declares herself so enamoured of her own beauty that she never cares for any other love.
- **Second Day** — "Qual donna canterá, s'io non canto io", sung by [[Person/pampinea]] with the others answering, while [[Person/emilia]] leads the carol: it celebrates a love fully returned.
- **Third Day** — "Niuna sconsolata", sung by [[Person/lauretta]] "with a very sweet voice, but in a manner somewhat plaintive": the lament of a woman who is beautiful and unloved in return. It was understood "differently by different people".
- **Fourth Day** — "Lagrimando dimostro", sung by [[Person/filostrato]] at the queen's command, while [[Person/filomena]] leads the dance: he grieves at having been betrayed by "Love under pledge of faith".
- **Fifth Day** — "Amor, la vaga luce", sung by [[Person/dioneo]] after the queen has forbidden him the unseemly songs he kept proposing, while Emilia leads the dance.
- **Sixth Day** — "Amor, s'io posso uscir de' tuoi artigli", sung by [[Person/elissa]] at the king's wish, while [[Person/panfilo]] leads the dance: it calls for deliverance from the bonds of love. All marvelled at such words, without being able to discern the reason for them.
- **Seventh Day** — "Deh! lassa la mia vita!", sung by Filomena: the longing to find the beloved again made the brigata judge "that some new and pleasing love held Filomena fast".
- **Eighth Day** — "Tanto è, Amore, il bene", sung by Panfilo: it declares an amorous happiness that must be concealed, and all strove in vain to guess what he was keeping hidden.
- **Ninth Day** — "Io mi son giovanetta", sung by [[Person/neifile]] "with a clear and cheerful voice" on the king's behalf.
- **Tenth Day** — "S'Amor venisse senza gelosia", sung by [[Person/fiammetta]] while Lauretta leads the dance: she would be happy as none other if love did not bring jealousy with it.
