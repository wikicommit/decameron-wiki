The "three crowns" are [[Person/dante-alighieri]], [[Person/petrarch]] and [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]], considered together as the founding authors of Italian literature.

## Use

In humanist Florence, Boccaccio's figure is remembered with affection and veneration, as can already be seen in the letters of Coluccio Salutati or in the *Vita* by Giannozzo Manetti: it is in this context that, together with the biographies of Dante and Petrarch written by Leonardo Bruni, the cult of the "three Florentine crowns" takes shape.

The relations among the three authors were in fact asymmetrical and not without tensions. Boccaccio saw in Petrarch a *praeceptor* and was throughout his life a passionate devotee of Dante, promoting his work and his figure; Petrarch, for his part, felt indifference toward Dante, and Boccaccio took note of this, moving closer to his mentality. Boccaccio himself, having recognised Petrarch's superiority in the Latin language, cooled his own more than optimistic judgement of Dante during the humanist phase, without however abandoning his determination to spread the cult of Alighieri and his work in Florence.
