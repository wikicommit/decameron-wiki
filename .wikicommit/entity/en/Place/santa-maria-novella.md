Santa Maria Novella is the Florentine church in which the [[Chapter/first-day-introduction]] of the [[Book/decameron]] sets the meeting from which the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]] is born. [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]] designates it as "the venerable church of Santa Maria Novella" and sets the scene there while the city, struck by the [[Event/black-death-1348]], is "almost void of inhabitants".

## Geography and places

The church is at [[Place/florence]] and is indicated in the text without further topographical precision. The place to which the company will remove after being formed there is described instead by contrast and without a name: a little hill distant not more than two small miles from the city, somewhat remote on every side from the roads, upon whose summit rises a palace with a fair and great courtyard in the midst, with loggias, halls and chambers adorned with joyful paintings, meadows round about, marvellous gardens, wells of the freshest waters and vaults of precious wines.

## History and significance

In the narrative the church is not a mere background, but the place in which the initiative of the characters takes shape. On a Tuesday morning, there being almost no other person present and the divine offices having been heard, seven young women come together there in mourning habit, all bound one to another by friendship, neighbourhood or kinship, none above twenty-eight years nor less than eighteen, "each of them discreet and of noble blood and fair of form and adorned in manners and of graceful modesty". Led by no set purpose but gathered by chance in one of the parts of the church, they seat themselves almost in a circle and, leaving off the saying of paternosters, begin to discourse of the quality of the time: from this conversation proceeds the speech of [[Person/pampinea]] who proposes to abandon the city.

It is still in the church that the three young men — [[Person/panfilo]], [[Person/filostrato]] and [[Person/dioneo]] — enter while these reasonings are going on among the women, and in it the agreement is concluded: Pampinea rises to her feet, greets them, makes known to them the disposition of the women and prays them to keep the group company; the young men, after a first moment in which they believe themselves mocked, accept and, even before departing, give order for what is needful for the setting out. Under this aspect Santa Maria Novella is the point of origin of the [[DefinedTerm/frame-narrative]] of the work.

## The place of leave-taking

Santa Maria Novella is also the place in which the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]] dissolves, fifteen days after being formed there. The return to the city having been resolved upon by the king [[Person/panfilo]], and all their belongings having been sent away by the steward, the company sets out again towards [[Place/florence]] as soon as the new day appears: the three young men leave the seven women "in Santa Maria Novella, whence they had departed with them", and having taken their leave of them attend to their other pleasures; the women, when it seems to them time, return to their own houses.

The church thus marks the two extremes of the frame narrative of the work, which begins and ends in the same place without the text describing what the company finds upon its return.
