The *Filocolo* is a prose romance by [[Person/giovanni-boccaccio]], composed in the Neapolitan period and dated to 1336-38, indicated elsewhere in the source as 1336-1339. The title, according to a rough etymology, means "labour of love". The work represents a turning point with respect to the romances of the origins, which were written in verse, and is identified as the first romance in the Italian vernacular.

## Plot

The story has as its protagonists Florio, son of a Saracen king, and Biancifiore (or Biancofiore), a Christian slave abandoned as a child. The two children grow up together and, once grown, fall in love after reading Ovid's *Ars amandi*, as had happened to Paolo and Francesca after reading of Guinevere and Lancelot. Florio's father, however, decides to separate them, selling Biancifiore to merchants. Florio therefore resolves to go in search of her and, after a thousand vicissitudes — whence the title *Filocolo* — meets her again. In the end the young man converts to Christianity and marries the maiden.

## Composition and Significance

The *Filocolo* is one of the clearest examples of [[DefinedTerm/boccaccian-experimentalism]]: the title is a Grecism; the narrative takes up the love story of Florio and Biancifiore, bound to the Occitan tradition; and classicising influences after the model of the *Historia distructionis Troiae* by Guido delle Colonne are at work in it. Compared with the [[Book/filostrato]], whose language is difficult and lofty, that of the *Filocolo* is highly superabundant.

The work is also a direct source for the author's literary biography: it is in the *Filocolo* that Boccaccio reports the details of his first meeting with [[Person/fiammetta]], stating that it took place on the Saturday of Holy Week in "a gracious and fair temple in Parthenope", that is, in the basilica dedicated to San Lorenzo in [[Place/naples]]. Boccaccio's European success was bound not only to the [[Book/decameron]] but also to works considered "minor" such as this one.
