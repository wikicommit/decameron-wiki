Emilia is one of the seven young women of the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]] of the [[Book/decameron]]. Her name has been interpreted both as "the flattering one" and as "she who is proud of her beauty".

Like [[Person/neifile]], Emilia is a very timid woman and has very little prominence within the brigata: together with [[Person/fiammetta]] and [[Person/lauretta]] she plays no active role and never intervenes in the group's choices, so much so that she may be considered a secondary figure with respect to [[Person/pampinea]], [[Person/filomena]] and [[Person/elissa]].

## Role in the brigata

Emilia is the little queen of the Ninth Day and chooses to give no theme at all: the Ninth Day is indeed, together with the First, one of the two days of free subject in the [[Book/decameron]].

## Her reign

Emilia governs the Ninth Day, and is the only sovereign of the [[DefinedTerm/brigata]] not to impose any theme. At the end of the Eighth, [[Person/lauretta]] places the laurel upon her head, saying in womanly fashion: "My lady, I know not how pleasing a queen we shall have of you, but a fair one we shall certainly have: see to it, then, that your works answer to your beauties". Emilia is a little ashamed — not so much at being made queen as at seeing herself publicly praised for that of which women are wont to be most desirous — and becomes in the face "such as at dawn are the new roses".

Her deliberation is motivated by two similitudes. The first is that of the oxen: after they have laboured "confined beneath the yoke", they are seen loosed and let go freely to pasture through the woods. The second is that of gardens, which are seen "to be no less fair, but much more so [...] leafy with divers plants, than the woods wherein we see only oaks". Having regard to how many days have been spent in discourse "restrained under a certain law", she deems that to wander somewhat — and in wandering to take back strength for returning beneath the yoke — is "not only useful but opportune"; therefore she lets each discourse "according as it pleases him", holding that the variety will be no less gracious. She observes finally that, having done thus, whoever succeeds her in the realm will be able to restrain the company within the accustomed laws "with greater surety", as being "the stronger". Each commends her "as a wise woman".
